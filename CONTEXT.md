# Live2DAction Flight System

Movement and combat systems for a third-person open-world action game (Unity), with an open-world traversal layer inspired by Genshin Impact / Wuthering Waves.

## Language

**Flight**:
The player's freely-controlled airborne movement mode — hold the flight key to ascend, hold the descend key to sink, release both to hover in place. Fully overrides gravity while active; ends on landing or on Flight Energy depletion (see Glide).
_Avoid_: Flying, gliding (Flight is the *active, energy-costing* mode; Glide is its fallback, a distinct state)

**Glide**:
The fallback airborne state entered when Flight Energy depletes mid-flight — a soft, reduced-gravity descent (not a hard fall), with horizontal movement still fully controllable. Costs no Flight Energy — Flight Energy regenerates normally while gliding, so Flight can be re-entered once it refills. Ends on landing.
_Avoid_: Falling (Falling is the normal, full-gravity airborne state outside of Flight/Glide entirely — e.g. walking off a ledge without ever having flown)

**Flight Energy**:
A dedicated regen-over-time resource, separate from the Ultimate skill's own energy, that Flight drains while active. Reaching zero ends Flight and starts Glide.

**Decorative Wings**:
A rigidly-attached, non-gameplay wing mesh with a procedural flapping animation, worn by a character for visual characterization only — grants that character no Flight/Glide ability and requires no AI changes. Used by the player (functional counterpart, worn alongside real Flight). Player4's mechanical wings were Decorative Wings only at first, but see Aerial Combat below - that scope decision has since been superseded.
_Avoid_: Flight wings (misleadingly implies functional flight, which Decorative Wings explicitly do not grant)

**Aerial Combat**:
Player4's AI-driven airborne engagement mode, entered reactively when the player is significantly higher than Player4. Distinct from the player's own Flight/Glide (no Flight Energy, no player-key-driven ascend/descend) - Player4 hovers/chases using its own simplified vertical-velocity control instead. Melee attacks during Aerial Combat reuse the player's existing EnemyAttack1-4 data unchanged, just with a spherical (not capsule) hit-judgment shape for vertical tolerance.
_Avoid_: Flight (Flight specifically means the player's energy-costing, key-driven mode - Player4 never has Flight Energy and isn't Flying in that sense, even while airborne fighting)

## Decisions

- **Takeoff**: No dedicated takeoff/transition animation — Flight begins the instant the flight key is held (matches Wuthering Waves' instant-lift-off model, not Genshin's jump-then-deploy-glider model).
- **Camera**: `ThirdPersonCameraController` pulls its follow distance back (~1.3–1.5×) while `IsFlying`, still subject to the existing obstruction-avoidance clamp. Angle/FOV unchanged for now.
- **Dive-to-build-momentum**: explicitly deferred. Flight stays a flat ascend/descend/hover model (no momentum tracking) until the basic version has actually been played and confirmed to feel right.
- **Player4's Decorative Wings flap rate**: tied to `EnemyAI.CurrentState` (idle rate normally, faster/harder flap while `Attacking`) rather than a constant rate — reuses `WingFlap`'s existing two-tier speed design, just driven by a different source than the player's `IsFlying`.
- **Aerial Combat partner**: Player4 (identified by its own large `detectionRange`, 8, vs 076's 3) — supersedes the earlier "Player4 is Decorative-Wings-only, no aerial AI" scope decision above. 076/077 explicitly excluded (Live2D billboard characters - flying them through 3D space would look wrong).
- **Player4's airborne movement**: an independent, simplified AI hover/vertical-chase (own vertical-velocity control, same shape as the existing `ApplyUpwardLaunch` hook) - does NOT share the player's Flight/Glide/Flight Energy system. No energy limit; AI just needs to reliably reach the target altitude.
- **Aiming at a vertically-offset target**: two changes together, not one alone - (a) characters gain real pitch rotation when facing a locked/aerial target, clamped to ±60°, eased the same way existing yaw turning already is; (b) melee hit-judgment switches from the standard forward capsule to a sphere centered on `attackOrigin` for vertical tolerance, so a slightly-off pitch angle still lands.
- **Spherical hit-judgment scope**: Aerial Combat only. Ground melee (both Player4's and the player's) keeps the existing forward-capsule judgment exactly as tuned - not touched by this feature.
- **Aerial Combat trigger**: player is >3m above Player4 (roughly 1.5-2 character heights) while Player4 can otherwise detect them. Below that, Player4's existing ground chase/attack logic is completely untouched.
- **Aerial Combat chase ceiling**: 15m. Beyond that Player4 abandons the chase, drifts back down to the ground, and waits to re-trigger if the player re-enters range. Purely a safety valve against the AI chasing the player out of the level/into the sky island's special geometry.
- **Aerial Combat attack data**: reuses `EnemyAttack`/`EnemyAttack2/3/4` unchanged (same damage/frame data) - only the hit-judgment shape changes (see above), not the moveset. Matches the explicit request that melee stays the primary combat mechanic in the air.
- **Aerial Combat exit threshold ≠ enter threshold**: real playtested bug - the first implementation exited Aerial Combat at the same 3m band used to enter it, giving zero hysteresis. That produced a rapid enter/exit bounce right at 3m (climb under aerial control → hit 3m → drop to gravity → fall → gap grows past 3m → re-enter → repeat), which read as an erratic flight trajectory and, worse, meant Player4 always disengaged the vertical-tolerant spherical judgment 3m short of actually reaching melee range - it could never get close enough to land a hit. Fixed by exiting only once the height gap has closed to within Player4's own effective attack range (not the 3m entry band) - it now keeps climbing/descending under aerial control all the way down to real melee distance before handing off to ground combat.
- **Pitch never rotates the CharacterController's own transform, for either character**: second real playtested bug, found right after the one above. Both CharacterMovement and EnemyAI originally applied their pitch angle directly to `transform.rotation` - but that's the SAME transform the CharacterController capsule lives on, and the capsule's "up" axis follows the transform's local Y axis. Pitching it doesn't aim the head, it physically tips the whole collision capsule over: visibly flickered between standing and lying flat as pitch changed frame to frame, and the tilted capsule's collision response fought vertical climb enough that Player4 could never gain net altitude on the player. Fixed by keeping the CharacterController's own transform yaw-only always, and instead applying pitch to the "Visual" child transform's local rotation (holds only the Animator, no CharacterController) - same visual look-up cue, capsule never leaves upright. (A related sign bug was caught and fixed in the same pass: `Quaternion.Euler(+X,0,0)` tips forward DOWN in Unity, opposite of AimUtility's "positive pitch = looking up" convention - the original code had never negated for this, so the player's `_visual` now correctly does `Euler(-pitch, 0, 0)`.)
- **Aerial Combat pitch is cosmetic only, not attack-judgment-relevant**: confirmed by the fixes above - `UseSphericalJudgment`'s hit check is a position-only sphere with no facing dependency, and the exit-hysteresis fix already guarantees `_isAerialCombat` is false (so facing has already reverted to horizontal) by the same frame an attack could ever land. Visual pitch exists purely so Player4 visibly looks up/down at the target mid-chase - it was never load-bearing for whether hits actually register.
- **Flight resume from Glide needs its own hysteresis too**: real playtested bug ("飛行有能耗 非一半就停下來導致飛行軌跡奇異"), same family as the Aerial Combat one above but on the player's own Flight/Glide toggle - this project's tuning has Flight draining energy faster (20/sec) than it regenerates (10/sec), so the original "resume Flight the instant CurrentEnergy > 0f" check let a single regen tick (10 energy) immediately re-enter Flight, drain back to 0 in 0.5s, and drop back to Glide - a repeating hop/glide stutter once energy first ran out, not a clean single transition. Fixed with `flightResumeEnergyThreshold` (30, i.e. Glide must bank a real reserve, not just >0) before Flight can resume - mirrors the Aerial Combat exit-threshold fix's own reasoning (asymmetric enter/exit values to kill an oscillation at a shared boundary).
- **Aerial Combat must keep tracking through its own swing, not just up to it**: real playtested bug ("敵人飛行時如果攻擊玩家會停留在原地 導致跟不上玩家 最後一直卡在飛行-攻擊落空-飛行的狀態") - the exit-hysteresis fix above stopped it from bailing out early, but once `CurrentState == Attacking` it still froze in place entirely (ground melee's own "target isn't going anywhere mid-swing" assumption, which doesn't hold against a fully 3D-mobile flying player). Fixed by (a) not exiting Aerial Combat while `combat.CurrentPhase != Idle` (a swing genuinely in progress), and (b) letting horizontal chase continue during Attacking too whenever `_isAerialCombat` is true - so it keeps adjusting position for the swing's ENTIRE duration and only actually lands once both close AND between swings.
- **Execution damage is now a fraction of CurrentHealth, not MaxHealth**: changed from the original "50% of MaxHealth" decision (explicit user request, "斬殺時改為扣除對方當前血量50%") - an execution now always takes a proportional bite out of however much fight is actually left in the target, rather than a fixed MaxHealth-based amount that becomes a shrinking fraction of remaining HP as a fight wears on. Renamed field on both ExecutionAbility/EnemyExecutionAbility to `executionDamagePercentOfCurrentHealth` to match.
- **Execution Ready Indicator**: a world-space red glow ring + blinking core dot, procedurally generated (not hand-authored art) and placed at chest height (offset forward off the root pivot so it renders in front of the torso instead of inside it - a real bug the first screenshot caught), driven by `StancePoise.IsStaggered` directly - visible only during the exact window a deathblow can land, on every character that has StancePoise (derived from the component itself, not a hardcoded name list).
- **Player4's Aerial Combat stats are 1.2x/1.5x the player's own flight stats, computed once, not live-coupled**: explicit user request ("讓敵人能夠飛得比角色高1.5倍 飛行速度比玩家快1.2倍"). `aerialCombatChaseCeiling` (45m) = 1.5x the player's own max CONTINUOUS Flight climb distance in one energy burst (flightAscendSpeed × maxEnergy/drainPerSecond = 30m at this project's tuning); `aerialVerticalSpeed` (7.2) = 1.2x the player's `flightAscendSpeed` (6); new dedicated `aerialHorizontalSpeed` (2.4) = 1.2x the player's `moveSpeed` (2), kept SEPARATE from Player4's own ground `moveSpeed` so the boost is flight-only, not also a ground-combat speed buff. Hardcoded numbers with the ratio reasoning in a comment, not a runtime cross-reference to CharacterMovement's fields - matches this session's existing precedent for every other direct-request stat tune (health/damage percentages etc.), and avoids a live coupling that would silently need to stay in sync if the player's own flight stats are retuned later.
- **Aerial Combat only truly exits once actually grounded, not merely "close enough"**: real playtested bug ("敵人擁有只會飛到玩家下方...請讓他一定要飛到玩家的身邊") - closing the height gap to melee range used to exit Aerial Combat outright (between swings, per the midSwing fix above), handing off to gravity with nothing underneath it in mid-air - so it spent most of an engagement sinking below the player and re-climbing rather than settling in beside them. Exit condition changed to `_controller.isGrounded` (plus the ceiling safety valve) - it now keeps hovering at the player's own altitude for the entire time it's airborne near them, only actually landing (and reverting to gravity) once the player themselves come back down and it genuinely touches ground.
- **Player4's Visual child never pitches, period**: follow-up real playtested bug ("敵人仍然斜斜的 要像玩家一樣站直才行") - a still-earlier fix had moved the aerial "look up/down at the target" pitch off the CharacterController's own transform onto the Animator-only "Visual" child instead, reasoning it was harmless since it never touched the collision capsule. That held while Aerial Combat only stayed active briefly (closing the gap) - but once the hover-stability fix above made it stay active for a WHOLE engagement, the same pitch became a near-constant visible tilt instead of an occasional look-up flourish. Removed entirely - Player4's body (both the root AND the Visual child) now stays fully upright always, matching how the player's own body reads (the player only ever pitches while deliberately locked onto a vertically-offset target, a rare gated action Player4 has no equivalent of). Also had to hard-reset Visual's `localRotation` back to identity once by hand - the many rounds of reflection-based verification this feature went through had left a stale non-zero rotation actually saved in the scene, which nothing would have self-corrected once the pitch-writing code was deleted.
- **Aerial vertical tracking is constant-rate pursuit (MoveTowards), not proportional decay**: follow-up real playtested bug ("敵人還是有機會卡在玩家下方且極限攻擊距離外導致空打") - the original vertical law was `velocity = clamp(heightDiff, -speed, speed)`, i.e. an exponential decay (~1s time constant) that slows down the closer it gets. Fine against a stationary target, but against a target that keeps repositioning (the whole premise of Aerial Combat), the last stretch of distance never actually finished closing - Player4 would sit at a small-but-nonzero residual gap indefinitely, often just outside effectiveAttackRange, causing whiffs. Switched to `Mathf.MoveTowards`-based pursuit: full aerialVerticalSpeed every frame until the remaining gap is smaller than one frame's own step, at which point it closes exactly (no overshoot, by MoveTowards's own guarantee) instead of asymptotically trending toward zero forever.
- **Two more edge cases found by a general review pass (not user-reported, caught proactively)**:
  - Aerial Combat's exit condition only ever checked vertical distance (ceiling) and grounded state, never horizontal distance - a player flying far away horizontally while staying elevated left Player4 with `CurrentState==Idle` (horizontal chase already frozen) but `_isAerialCombat` stuck true forever, hovering motionless in mid-air indefinitely instead of giving up and landing. Added `distance > detectionRange` as a third exit trigger (same `!midSwing` gate as the grounded check, so an already-committed swing still finishes).
  - Getting staggered while genuinely airborne (which Aerial Combat now makes common, given the hover-stability fix above keeps Player4 airborne for most of an engagement) used to still fall under gravity - horizontal velocity already froze while staggered (matches "stand frozen and open" intent) but vertical never did, so a "kneeling, about to be executed" enemy would plummet mid-pose instead of actually holding still. Vertical velocity now hard-freezes to 0 while staggered, airborne or not.
- **Health rebalance + slower stance growth**: explicit user request ("玩家血量改為 500 敵人改為400 同時減緩架勢條成長速度"). Player MaxHealth 300→500, Player4 MaxHealth 100→400. Stance/poise growth is now a separate rate from raw damage dealt - new `stanceGainMultiplier` (0.5, applied to every StancePoise-bearing character: Player/Player2/Player4) scales incoming damage before it's added to the poise bar, so a stagger now takes roughly twice as many hits at this project's common 10-damage attacks (6→12 hits to fill a 60 maxStance bar) - deliberately a separate multiplier from `maxStance` itself, since raising `maxStance` would also change how much accumulated damage a stagger represents, not just how fast it fills. Execution damage (percent of CurrentHealth) needed no corresponding change - it already scales automatically with whatever the target's current health happens to be.
- **Death Animation (Dying.fbx)**: explicit user request ("將這個動作作為所有角色死亡時的共同動作"). Real architectural conflict this surfaced: `Health.ApplyDamage` used to call `gameObject.SetActive(false)` SYNCHRONOUSLY in the same call that fires `Died` - meaning nothing ever got even one frame to render a death animation before the whole GameObject (and its Animator) vanished. Fixed with a new `Health.deferDeactivationToDeathAnimation` bool (default false, preserves the original instant-deactivation behavior for anything that doesn't opt in) - when true, `DeathAnimationLink` (living on the character, wired via a new `DeathAnimationSetup` bootstrap script) triggers the Animator's "Dead" state and deactivates the GameObject itself after the clip's own measured duration (3.5s) instead. Only wired onto Player/Player3/Player4 (the three sharing Maya's/Arisa's Humanoid Animator Controllers, both of which got a new terminal "Dead" state with no return transition to Locomotion) - Player2 (no Animator at all) and 076/077 (Live2D Cubism billboards, a different animation system entirely) keep the original immediate-deactivation path since a Humanoid FBX clip isn't applicable to either rig type. CharacterMovement/EnemyAI/PlayerCombat all needed a `health.IsDead` freeze added alongside their existing `staggered` freeze (a dead-but-not-yet-deactivated character would otherwise keep moving/attacking for those 3.5s) - kept as a SEPARATE `frozen = staggered || isDead` gate in EnemyAI specifically (not merged into `staggered` itself), since `CurrentState == Staggered` is StancePoise-specific AI-FSM bookkeeping that a dead enemy isn't actually in.
- **Uniform basic-attack damage (25) across Player/Player4/076**: explicit user request ("將玩家 敵人 076的普通攻擊傷害改為25 每段都是"). Every combo step's AttackData - LightAttack1-4 (Player, shared with Player3), EnemyAttack/2/3/4 (Player4), GiantAttack1/2/3 (076) - set to Damage=25 uniformly. EnemyUltimateAttack (30) deliberately left untouched - it's the ultimate skill, not a "普通攻擊" (basic attack), so it wasn't in scope.
- **Execution deferred-damage timing reconfirmed, not a new bug**: user asked to ensure execution damage only lands after the animation finishes, "所有人套用" (apply to everyone). Both `ExecutionAbility` (player→target) and `EnemyExecutionAbility` (Player4→player) already implemented this correctly from earlier session work (`_elapsed < executionAnimationSeconds` gates `ResolveExecution`) - reflection-verified both directions rather than assuming: health stays unchanged while pending and elapsed is far below the threshold, and only actually drops once elapsed crosses `executionAnimationSeconds`. No code change was needed here.
